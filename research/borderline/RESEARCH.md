# Borderline (BPD) research notes for Plan B

Written 2026-11-01. Purpose: the evidence behind every borderline text in Rafiq. **Nothing here is app copy.** Copy is drafted later, marked «مسودة للمراجعة», and reviewed by the owner's therapist.

How this was gathered: extended web searches, then direct reads of the official pages where the site allowed it. Pages I read in full are marked **read**. Pages known only from a search summary are marked **summary only** and must be opened and checked before their claims go into the app. Two official pages (psychiatryonline.org, nice.org.uk) refused automated reading (HTTP 403), so those claims rest on search summaries.

## 3. History (for the gentle illness-history text)

Usable skeleton, **with the dates still to verify against primary sources**:

- 1930s–1950s: psychoanalysts such as Adolph Stern describe patients on the «border» between neurosis and psychosis who did not fit existing groups. (Stern 1938; one source says 1937.)
- 1960s–1970s: Otto Kernberg describes «borderline personality organization»; Gunderson and colleagues turn it into a set of criteria in the late 1970s.
- 1980: «borderline personality disorder» enters the DSM-III.
- 1991 onward: Marsha Linehan's DBT is tested in a randomized trial and later replicated; MBT follows from Bateman and Fonagy.
- Modern view: ICD-11 (2019/2022) treats personality disorder by severity, with «borderline pattern» as an optional qualifier.

A note worth telling the reader honestly: the word «borderline» is a historical leftover that no longer describes the condition well; many clinicians and people with lived experience dislike it. This supports using wording like «اضطراب الشخصية الحدّية» without turning it into an identity.

## 4. Safety and language rules for the drafts

1. The app describes and supports. It never says or implies «you have BPD». Reading about symptoms is not a diagnosis.
2. Person-first wording («شخص يعاني من»), never «حدّي» as a noun for a person.
3. Never romanticize the illness, self-harm or suicide. «Strong terms that make a person love themselves» must stay on **self-worth, sensitivity as a human quality, and courage in healing**. They must not praise the pain or the illness itself.
4. Do not show suicide rates or scary statistics to readers. Show the hopeful, true findings together with their honest limits (C9 with C10).
5. Every page about symptoms or history ends with a path to help: the existing crisis page («أحتاج تهدئة») and the country contacts.
6. Self-compassion wording must be gentle and optional, because for some people it triggers shame (C12). Offer validation first, then choice.
7. Every text carries its source IDs in the data, shows them in the app, and is marked «مسودة للمراجعة» until the therapist approves it.
8. Arabic: plain modern standard Arabic, warm, no clinical shock words in headlines.
